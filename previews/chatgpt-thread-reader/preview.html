Can you explain what an eclipse is?
Assistant

Understanding eclipses

An eclipse happens when one celestial body blocks sunlight from reaching another, or passes into its shadow. From Earth, the two familiar kinds are solar eclipses and lunar eclipses.

During a solar eclipse, the Moon passes between Earth and the Sun. During a lunar eclipse, Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon, casting a shadow across the Moon.

Neither kind happens every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted relative to Earth's orbital plane.

What is an annular solar eclipse?
Assistant

The ring of fire

An annular eclipse occurs when the Moon passes in front of the Sun while appearing slightly smaller than the Sun. A thin bright ring of the Sun remains visible around its edge.

The Moon's distance from Earth varies over its orbit. When it is farther away, its apparent size can be too small to cover the Sun completely.

Never look directly at the Sun without correctly rated eclipse glasses, even during an annular eclipse.

How might someone safely observe one?
Assistant

Watching safely

Use ISO 12312-2 compliant eclipse viewers or indirect projection. Ordinary sunglasses, smoked glass, and exposed film are not safe replacements.

With a pinhole projector, let sunlight pass through a tiny opening onto a separate surface. View the projected image rather than looking into the Sun.

The timing and location determine which stages of an eclipse you can see, so check a reliable local eclipse map before planning your observation.
